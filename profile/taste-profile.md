# Shams — Visual & Architectural Taste Profile

## 01 — Core Thinking

Shams tends to value architecture as a system of relationships rather than as an isolated formal object.

Important qualities:

- A clear conceptual logic behind visual decisions
- Relationships between architecture, context, movement, behavior and material
- Novelty through logic rather than arbitrary formal experimentation
- Critical reinterpretation rather than literal imitation
- Visual ideas that carry architectural meaning
- Representation as part of the architectural argument, not merely presentation

Avoid:

- Generic architectural solutions
- Ready-made design formulas
- Concepts that could apply to any project
- Formal novelty without an underlying reason


## 02 — Architectural Attitude

Preferred architectural approaches tend to be:

- Contemporary
- Contextually aware but not nostalgic
- Critical toward regional architectural conventions
- Interested in everyday urban life
- Interested in publicness, movement and collective space
- Interested in ambiguity between building and city
- Comfortable with incomplete, open or adaptable spatial conditions

Traditional references should generally be transformed, abstracted or questioned rather than copied literally.


## 03 — Representation

Strong preference for:

- Minimal compositions
- Controlled visual hierarchy
- Monochrome or very limited palettes
- Strong contrast
- Precise linework
- Carefully controlled shadows
- White space
- Hybrid representation
- Physical model + digital drawing
- Massing + linework
- Photography + graphic intervention
- Abstract but spatially legible diagrams

Representation should have its own conceptual position.

A drawing should not simply explain the building.
The method of representation can itself communicate an architectural idea.


## 04 — Hybrid Representation

Particularly relevant visual language:

- 3D massing combined with architectural linework
- Raw white/clay model surfaces
- Partial transitions between physical-looking mass and drawing
- Selective linework rather than complete wireframes
- Different representation modes within one architectural object
- Clean boundaries between visual states
- Real depth and shadow combined with 2D information

Avoid conventional:

- Exploded axonometrics
- Generic wireframes
- Standard diagram sets
- Overly technical axonometrics
- Repetitive information diagrams


## 05 — Graphic Language

Preferred:

- Minimal
- Precise
- Quiet
- Spatial
- Editorial
- Conceptual
- Slightly unusual
- Controlled
- High information density without visual clutter

Graphic interventions should feel intentional rather than decorative.

Avoid:

- Excessive arrows
- Too many colors
- Decorative icons
- Stock illustrations
- Generic architectural pictograms
- Over-labelled diagrams
- Visually noisy collages


## 06 — Model Photography

Strong interest in:

- White architectural maquettes
- Raw massing
- Model photography
- Controlled natural/artificial shadows
- Minimal backgrounds
- Graphic overlays on physical models
- Water/reflection used conceptually
- Small interventions that alter the reading of the model

The model should remain recognizably architectural rather than becoming a generic CGI image.


## 07 — References

Prefer:

- Contemporary architecture offices
- Experimental architectural representation
- Artists with unusual spatial thinking
- Graphic designers
- Photographers
- Film directors
- Experimental visual culture

References should be evaluated by their underlying method, not merely by surface similarity.


## 08 — Research Method

For every visual reference, distinguish between:

1. Direct reference
2. Conceptual reference
3. Representation reference
4. Material reference
5. Superficial visual similarity

Do not treat superficial similarity as evidence of relevance.


## 09 — Critical Filter

Before recommending a reference, ask:

- Why is this relevant?
- What exactly is being borrowed?
- Is the similarity conceptual or merely visual?
- Does the reference introduce something genuinely new?
- Could the same recommendation be given to any architecture student?

If the last answer is yes, the reference is probably too generic.


## 10 — Evolving Profile

This document is intentionally incomplete.

Pinterest references should progressively reveal:

- Recurring visual patterns
- Preferred compositions
- Repeated architectural attitudes
- Preferred representation techniques
- Recurring material interests
- Preferred levels of abstraction
- Unexpected interests
- Changes in taste over time

New observations should be added only when supported by repeated evidence from the visual archive.

The profile should evolve rather than remain fixed.
